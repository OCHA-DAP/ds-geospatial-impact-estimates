Before a crisis

Done once, in advance. No event is needed for this.

  • Partners sign up on HDX

    Organisations that produce satellite damage assessments register once.

    HDX already lists organisations able to provide these assessments. Others can still join during a crisis.

    Partners

  • Partners say what they can provide

    The kind of assessment they usually produce, the method, and how soon after an event.

    So everyone knows what to expect from each partner.

    Partners

  • Partners are onboarded

    They agree on the minimum every assessment should come with, such as the area analysed and the image dates.

    Partners

  • Partners are trained

    A short walkthrough of how to upload, confirm and update, so nobody learns it in the middle of a crisis.

    Partners

A disaster happens Everything below is repeated for each event.

During a crisis

Activation

  1. 1

    A crisis page opens

    The A&A cell is activated and HDX opens a page for the event.

    Both already happen today. Everything after this step is new.

    A&A cell and HDX

  2. 2

    Partners are notified

    Every registered partner gets an email about the event.

    Platform

  3. 3

    Partners say if they are working on it

    A yes, a no, or no answer at all. All three are recorded.

    Partners

Collection

  1. 4

    Partners upload to HDX

    The assessment goes on the crisis page, together with the area that was analysed.

    The area analysed shows where someone looked and found nothing, and where nobody looked at all.

    Partners

  2. 5

    Partners confirm the data is ready

    A short form tells the platform where the files are. The platform then collects them.

    Details about the organisation and the product are entered once and remembered for later deliveries.

    PartnersPlatform

  3. 6

    Partners can send updates

    A new version replaces the last one. Earlier versions are kept.

    A partner can also submit more than one product for the same event.

    Output 1Data registry

    One table shows which assessments exist, who is working on what, and what has arrived.

    Interoperability

    1. 7

      Each delivery is checked

      A check on format and completeness. It does not judge which assessment is right.

      The partner hears back either way. If something is missing, the message says what.

      Platform

    2. 8

      Everything moves to a common base

      All assessments are placed on the same building map and the same damage scale.

      The original files stay available as delivered.

      Platform

    Output 2Interoperability of assessments

    The assessments can be read side by side, building by building.

    Points, areas and pixels become the same set of buildings.

    Joint analysis

    1. 9

      A joint analysis is produced

      The platform looks across all the assessments at once.

      It works as soon as a second assessment arrives and improves with each one after that.

      Platform

    For discussion. What the joint analysis should contain is open. The first three parts below are the ones the Venezuela evaluation points to.

    • Coverage

      Who looked where, and the gaps nobody covered.

    • Agreement

      How many assessments flag each building.

    • Ranking of areas

      Which areas come first, by administrative area or by neighbourhood cell. This is what responders need most in the first 72 hours.

      In Venezuela, ranking by agreement found 15 of the 20 worst hit cells. The best single assessment found 13.

    • InterpretationOpen

      A place for a written reading of the results, for example drafted with an AI language model. Another partner could lead this.

    Output 3Joint analysis

    Data and infrastructure

    The data will be publicly available on HDX. The scope is limited to non sensitive contexts. The processing can be developed on infrastructure approved by OICT. The three outputs are pages of one viewer. The planned address is datascience.unocha.org.
