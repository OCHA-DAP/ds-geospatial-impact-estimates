# Platform name: candidates

Status: open. The working name is "Damage Analysis Platform" (the hub under
`pages/damage-analysis-platform/`). This note keeps the candidates and the
reasoning so the decision is not re-derived from scratch. Last edited 2026-11-25.

## What the name has to cover

The platform has three outputs, and a name that covers only one of them reads
wrong for the other two:

1. a **registry** of organisations and the products they can deliver (before a crisis);
2. **interoperable assessments**: a common building map and damage scale, so
   different providers' products can be compared (during a crisis);
3. a **joint analysis** per event: coverage, agreement, ranking of areas,
   interpretation (the Venezuela 2026 story is the worked example).

Two sentences every candidate must survive, because they are what the site
actually says to people:

- "Register your organisation on the ___."
- "The ___ for the Venezuela earthquake is published."

The first needs a system. The second needs an output. A name that only passes
the second is naming the joint analysis, not the platform.

Conventions worth following: OCHA names that stuck are plain and say what the
thing is (HDX, ReliefWeb, CODAB, JIAF). Avoid an invented acronym that needs
explaining, avoid "satellite" (field and crowd assessments are in the registry
too), and avoid partner names (IMPACT, Pulse) and sensing methods (radar).

## Candidates

### Descriptive

| Name | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinated Damage Assessment (CDA) | all three | Names the activity, not the software. Passes both sentences once a system noun is implied ("register on CDA"). Already in use in discussion. |
| Joint Damage Assessment (JDA) | all three | "Joint" is the UN word for a multi-partner exercise; JIAF is the direct precedent (registry of inputs + standards + joint output). Dry in the way HNO and JIAF are dry. |
| Joint Damage Assessment Framework (JDAF) | all three | Same, read as a methodology partners sign up to, with the site as its implementation. Could be confused with the team's anticipatory-action frameworks. |
| Damage Assessment Coordination Hub | all three | Honest about the function. Weak handle, no clean acronym. |
| Damage Assessment Registry | 1 | True today; too small once the joint analysis is the main event. |

### "Common ..." family

"Common" borrows "common operational picture", a term responders already know,
and reads as shared rather than owned. Its weakness is that what is common
between assessments is output 3, so the word favours the joint analysis over the
registry and the standards.

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| Common Damage Picture | Best as the name of the per-event joint analysis. Fails the register sentence. Initials CDP are taken several times over, so never shorten it. |
| Common Damage Atlas | An atlas is a collection of maps, so it covers the registry as well as the output; passes both sentences. Initials CDA match "coordinated damage assessment". |
| Common Damage Map | Short, concrete. Weaker for the registry (a map is one thing). CDM is the UN carbon mechanism. |
| Common Damage Layer | GIS vocabulary, says the assessments are stacked. Reads cold outside GIS. |

### Backronyms that spell a related word

| Name | Expansion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MOSAIC | Multi-Organisation Structural Assessment Integration and Coordination | Many tiles, one picture. "Structural" puts building damage in the expansion; with "Shared" instead it sounds like any assessment. Popular word; check for clashes in the UN and humanitarian space before adopting. |
| RADAR | Registry of Assessments for Damage Agreement and Ranking | Maps one-to-one onto the three outputs. Radar is also a sensing method in the registry, so some will assume it is a SAR product. |
| MERGE | Multi-source Estimate Registry for Geospatial Evaluation | Plain and systemic, less memorable. |
| CoDA | Coordinated Damage Assessment | Real word, but unrelated to damage, and one letter from CODAB. |
| GRID | Geospatial Registry of Impact and Damage | Tight, but taken inside the UN by UNEP/GRID-Geneva. |

### Single words (the agreement idea)

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| Quorum | The k-of-n slider is literally a quorum: how many assessments must agree before a building counts. Precise, explains the method every time it is said. |
| Concord | Agreement in one word; people think of the place or the plane. |
| Chorus | Many voices, one signal; says nothing about damage or geography. |
| Triangulate | Analysts' vocabulary plus the geospatial wink; works better as a tagline than a name. |
| Tally | What the agreement map does per building; undersells coordination. |

## Current leaning

Two names at two levels, the way HDX (system) and HNO (output) coexist:

- a plain, systemic name for the platform: Joint Damage Assessment or
  Coordinated Damage Assessment, with the subtitle "coordinated damage
  assessment" fixed under whichever wordmark is chosen;
- "common damage picture" as the plain-language name of the per-event joint
  analysis, written in full.

Nothing is decided. Whoever picks should run the two test sentences above and
search for clashes before changing the hub title, the hostname request, or the
KB page.
